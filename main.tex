\begin{abstract}
% Land subsidence develops as groundwater withdrawal increases effective stress and compacts susceptible sediments within an aquifer system. Surface displacement measurements describe the total vertical response, but they do not show how the compaction is distributed with depth. Multilayer compaction monitoring wells provide this information directly, although their monthly observations may not be available when a current assessment is required. This study tested whether routinely available monitoring data could provide provisional estimates of monthly compaction during a six-month delay in the delivery of multilayer compaction observations at Tuku, central Taiwan. The analysis represented the monitored profile by six 50~m depth sections and related their monthly compaction to hydraulic head changes, vertical surface displacement from a continuous Global Navigation Satellite System station, and seasonal terms. Effective management of land subsidence caused by groundwater withdrawal requires information on how aquifer-system compaction is distributed with depth. Multilayer compaction monitoring well (MLCW) measurements provide this information directly, but processing and quality control can delay finalized records by several months. This study evaluated a monitoring framework that combined hydraulic head changes and total vertical displacement measured by a continuous Global Navigation Satellite System (cGNSS) station to estimate monthly deformation at depth during such delays. Secondary evaluations considered 3, 5, or 8 years of monthly MLCW observations followed by measurements every six or twelve months. A limiting case examined estimates without subsequent MLCW updates. Separate Bayesian ridge regression models related monthly deformation in six 50~m depth sections to hydraulic head changes, total vertical displacement, and seasonal variation. Earlier MLCW deformation established these relations but was not used to estimate later months. During a six-month delay, mean absolute error (MAE) ranged from 0.15 to 0.26~mm/month in the four shallowest sections and from 0.40 to 0.50~mm/month in the two deepest. With MLCW measurements every six or twelve months, average monthly MAE remained within 0.27--0.31~mm/month across the six sections. Models established from 5 or 8 years of monthly MLCW observations did not consistently achieve lower monthly errors than those established from 3 years. Without subsequent MLCW updates, monthly errors did not increase consistently across the depth sections, but mean absolute cumulative discrepancy reached 4.3--7.4~mm after more than 6 years. Across all three evaluations, the 90\% posterior predictive intervals contained fewer observations than intended and therefore did not fully represent uncertainty. Hydraulic head changes and total vertical displacement can therefore support monthly estimates of deformation at depth between MLCW observations. The framework compares candidate schedules at individual sites through monthly error, accumulated discrepancy, and posterior interval coverage, while periodic MLCW observations remain necessary to measure accumulated deformation and update the fitted relations.
\end{abstract}
