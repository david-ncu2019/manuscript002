\subsection{Reduced-observation sensitivity settings}
\label{app:no_update_settings}

Rolling origins coincided with the start of the existing six-month evaluation blocks, producing 22 origins spaced six months apart from May 2013 through November 2023. At each origin, the Bayesian ridge regression model was fitted once using MLCW responses available before that date. The fitted model estimated compaction for the following twelve months without receiving an additional MLCW observation and without being fitted again. The first six months of each twelve-month estimate series defined the six-month horizon from the same model fit. Consecutive twelve-month horizons therefore overlapped by six months.

The endpoint error was defined as the cumulative estimate minus the cumulative MLCW observation over the corresponding horizon, $e=\hat{y}-y$. A positive endpoint error represented an estimate that was more positive than the observed cumulative value and therefore indicated underestimated compaction magnitude when the observed value was negative. MAE, RMSE, and mean signed error were reported in millimeters. The horizon-normalized MAE was calculated by dividing MAE by six or twelve months and was reported in millimeters per month. The coefficient of determination was excluded because the analysis compared cumulative endpoint errors rather than individual monthly estimates. The monthly prediction intervals described in \Cref{subsec:uncertainty} were not combined across either horizon.

Ninety-five percent confidence intervals for each summary statistic and for the difference between the twelve- and six-month horizon-normalized MAE were calculated with a paired moving-block bootstrap. The ordered sequence of 22 origins was resampled 2,000 times with a block length of two origins. Each resample used the same origins for both horizons, which preserved their paired comparison and accounted for the six-month overlap between consecutive twelve-month horizons.
