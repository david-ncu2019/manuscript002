Monthly compaction estimates were evaluated under three conditions of MLCW data availability. The primary condition delayed complete monthly records for six months, whereas two reduced-frequency conditions provided one cumulative observation every six or twelve months. Results are presented with their practical implications because each condition provides a different amount of direct subsurface information.

\subsection{Sensitivity to MLCW measurements collected every six months}
\label{subsec:discussion_reduced_sampling}
\label{subsec:results_sparse_h6}

GWL and cGNSS observations remained available each month, while the endpoint MLCW observation constrained the accumulated compaction before the next interval was estimated.

\subsection{Sensitivity to MLCW measurements collected every twelve months}
\label{subsec:results_sparse_h12}

The twelve-month analysis followed the same sequence but provided one cumulative MLCW observation after every twelve estimated months. The first reduced-frequency measurements occurred at months 48, 72, and 108 for the 36-, 60-, and 96-month initial calibration periods. Each scenario then continued through complete twelve-month intervals ending no later than the final month shared by the MLCW, GWL, and cGNSS records.

Their cumulative endpoint MAEs were \placeholder{SPARSE H12 TRAIN36 ENDPOINT MAE WITH UNIT}, \placeholder{SPARSE H12 TRAIN60 ENDPOINT MAE WITH UNIT}, and \placeholder{SPARSE H12 TRAIN96 ENDPOINT MAE WITH UNIT}, respectively. The interval-level values in \Cref{fig:sparse_interval_sensitivity} further show \placeholder{SPARSE H12 ERROR DISTRIBUTION PATTERN}.

The twelve-month results indicate \placeholder{H12 INTERPRETATION}. Longer intervals provide fewer direct constraints on how compaction is distributed through time, even though monthly GWL and cGNSS observations remain available. The analysis was limited to one monitoring site, and the three initial calibration periods ended in different calendar years. Differences among the 36-, 60-, and 96-month scenarios therefore reflect both the amount of initial MLCW information and the hydrogeological conditions during their subsequent evaluation periods. They should not be interpreted as the isolated causal effect of calibration length or transferred directly to other MLCW stations.
