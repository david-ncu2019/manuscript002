Monthly deformation increments were estimated under the three monitoring conditions defined in \Cref{subsec:experimental_design}. These conditions represented temporary delays in finalized monthly MLCW records, less frequent MLCW field measurements, and the absence of subsequent MLCW measurements. Each condition removed a different amount of direct subsurface information and therefore addressed a separate monitoring question. The results are presented in the same order as the experimental designs, followed by the fitted coefficients and the limitations of the analysis.

\subsection{Monthly estimation during delayed MLCW data delivery}
\label{subsec:results_delayed_delivery}

The recurring estimation and update design produced 23 complete six-month cycles from 05/2013 to 10/2024. As shown in \Cref{fig:results_delayed_cycles}, the calibration record expanded after the finalized MLCW observations from each cycle became available. Each cycle contributed six monthly estimates for each of the six depth sections, resulting in 138 estimates per section and 828 estimates across the monitored profile.

The observed and estimated monthly increments followed similar temporal patterns in the upper four sections, whereas larger differences occurred in S5 and S6 (\Cref{fig:results_delayed_monthly_estimates}). The same contrast appears in \Cref{fig:results_delayed_scatter}, where estimates for S1--S4 remained closer to the 1:1 line than those for the two deepest sections. Performance differed substantially with depth (\Cref{tab:delayed_performance_interval}). The pooled estimates reached $R^2=0.778$, RMSE~$=0.423$~mm/month, and MAE~$=0.279$~mm/month. Section-level $R^2$ ranged from 0.076 at S5 to 0.976 at S2. RMSE ranged from 0.212~mm/month at S1 to 0.661~mm/month at S5, whereas MAE ranged from 0.153~mm/month at S3 to 0.504~mm/month at S5. Thus, $R^2$ and the error magnitudes described complementary aspects of performance rather than a common ranking among sections.

Every estimate carried a 90\% Bayesian posterior predictive interval obtained from the fitted posterior as defined in \Cref{subsec:predictive_uncertainty}. Pooled empirical coverage was 78.019\%, which was 11.981 percentage points below the nominal level, and the mean interval width was 0.904~mm/month. Coverage ranged from 65.942\% at S6 to 88.406\% at S2 and did not follow the section-level ordering of $R^2$. S5 had the largest point errors and the widest mean interval at 1.848~mm/month, indicating that its estimates were both less accurate and more uncertain than those of the other sections.

Performance was also grouped by position within each estimation cycle to determine whether the estimates changed as additional months passed without finalized MLCW records (\Cref{tab:delayed_performance_by_month}). MAE varied from 0.252 to 0.336~mm/month, while RMSE varied from 0.352 to 0.593~mm/month. The largest errors occurred in the second month rather than at the end of the cycle. Mean error remained between $-0.013$ and 0.048~mm/month, and empirical coverage varied between 73.188\% and 82.609\%. These results show no monotonic loss of performance across the six monthly positions.

The lower performance at S5 is consistent with limited section-specific hydraulic information. Hydraulic head was measured directly at only three depths at Tuku, while values for the remaining standardized sections were obtained by kriging from the regional network (\Cref{subsec:predictor_preparation}). This interpretation remains conditional because the present analysis did not isolate the contribution of groundwater monitoring depth from the other predictors. More generally, each monthly estimate used hydraulic head and cGNSS information available through the target month, including the specified lagged changes. The evaluation therefore represents monthly estimation during delayed MLCW data delivery rather than forecasting conditions that had not yet been observed. The coverage shortfall and its implications are considered further in \Cref{subsec:results_limitations}.
