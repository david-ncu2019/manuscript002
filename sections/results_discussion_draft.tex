Monthly compaction estimates were evaluated under the three monitoring conditions defined in \Cref{subsec:experimental_design}. The first condition delays complete monthly MLCW records temporarily, the second reduces MLCW measurement frequency to once every six or twelve months, and the third stops MLCW reporting permanently after 3, 5, or 8 years. Each condition removes a different amount of direct subsurface information, and the three conditions are reported separately because they draw on different frozen model snapshots and answer different operational questions. The fitted coefficients are examined afterwards, followed by the limitations that bound every result reported here.

\subsection{Monthly estimation during delayed MLCW data delivery}
\label{subsec:results_delayed_delivery}

The recurring estimation and update cycle defined in \Cref{subsec:delayed_evaluation} produced 138 monthly estimates for each depth section across 23 complete six-month estimation periods, for a pooled total of 828 estimates (\Cref{tab:delayed_performance_interval}). Pooled across the six sections, the estimates reached $R^2=0.778$, RMSE~$=0.423$~mm/month, and MAE~$=0.279$~mm/month. Section-level $R^2$ ranged from 0.076 at the 200--250~m section (S5) to 0.976 at the 50--100~m section (S2). Three sections fell between these values, with $R^2=0.888$ at S1, 0.870 at S3, and 0.798 at S4, and the 250--300~m section (S6) reached $R^2=0.337$. The error magnitudes followed the same ordering, with RMSE ranging from 0.212~mm/month at S1 to 0.661~mm/month at S5, and MAE from 0.153~mm/month at S3 to 0.504~mm/month at S5. \Cref{fig:results_delayed_monthly_estimates} shows the observed and estimated increments for each section across the full evaluation period.

Every estimate carried a 90\% Bayesian posterior predictive interval obtained from the fitted posterior as defined in \Cref{subsec:predictive_uncertainty}, so an interval accompanied the first eligible estimate without requiring an archive of previous errors. Pooled empirical coverage reached 78.019\%, approximately twelve percentage points below the nominal 90\% level, with a mean interval width of 0.904~mm/month. Coverage varied among sections from 65.942\% at S6 to 88.406\% at S2, and only S2 and S4 (84.058\%) approached the nominal level. Coverage did not follow the ordering of $R^2$. S5 combined the weakest point accuracy with 81.159\% coverage and the widest mean interval of the six sections at 1.848~mm/month, exceeding the 0.985~mm/month at S4 by a factor of 1.877.

Two features of these results carry operational meaning. The weak accuracy at S5 is consistent with a physical observability gap rather than a limitation of the fitted relation. The deepest groundwater well at Tuku is screened at 257--263~m and serves as the hydraulic head predictor for both S5 and S6 (\Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}), so no piezometer observes head within the 200--250~m interval that S5 represents, and the predictor available there describes pore-pressure conditions in a deeper interval than the one undergoing compaction. Where a monitoring network no longer observes hydraulic head within an actively compacting layer, neither surface displacement nor head data recorded elsewhere in the profile restores the ability to estimate compaction in that layer. The second feature concerns timing. Because each estimate used only hydraulic head and cGNSS observations recorded in the same target month, the capability demonstrated here is same-month estimation rather than forecasting, and its operational value lies in bridging the interval between a target month and the delayed delivery of the corresponding MLCW record. \Cref{subsec:results_limitations} returns to the coverage shortfall.
