Monthly deformation within individual depth sections may be unavailable when finalized MLCW records are delayed, when measurements are made less often, or when subsequent observations cease. At Tuku, separate Bayesian ridge regression models related monthly deformation increments in six 50~m depth sections to hydraulic head changes, total vertical displacement measured by cGNSS, and seasonal variation, while MLCW observations supplied the deformation values used to establish and assess these relations. During a six-month delay, the estimates agreed more closely with the observed deformation increments in S1--S4 than in S5 and S6, with $R^2$ values of 0.80--0.98 and MAE values of 0.15--0.26~mm/month in the four shallower sections. The 90\% Bayesian posterior predictive intervals contained 65.9--88.4\% of the observations during delayed delivery, and average coverage remained below 90\% in every scenario with less frequent or no subsequent MLCW measurements.

When MLCW measurements were made every six or twelve months, average monthly MAE remained within 0.27--0.31~mm/month across the tested measurement intervals and initial records. The discrepancy accumulated by the next MLCW measurement was larger over the longer interval, with average endpoint MAE of 1.4--1.54~mm at six months and 2.47--2.74~mm at twelve months. Without subsequent MLCW observations, average monthly MAE remained within 0.26--0.29~mm/month, while average absolute cumulative error after 80 months ranged from 4.29 to 7.39~mm and did not remain lowest for the longest initial record throughout the evaluation period or across every depth section. These findings distinguish agreement for individual months from the discrepancy that accumulates before deformation is measured directly again. Rather than identifying a universal measurement interval or a preferred length for the initial record, monthly error, accumulated discrepancy, and posterior interval coverage provide evidence for comparing candidate schedules at each site. By combining monthly hydraulic head observations and total vertical displacement measured by cGNSS through Bayesian ridge regression, the framework provides monthly estimates of deformation between MLCW observations, while subsequent MLCW measurements supply the direct depth-specific evidence needed to assess accumulated deformation.
