Current information on monthly deformation within individual depth sections may be unavailable when finalized MLCW records are delayed or MLCW measurements are recorded less often, and the gap persists if later measurements cease altogether. At Tuku, separate Bayesian ridge regression models estimated monthly deformation increments for six 50~m depth sections. Each model related monthly MLCW deformation increments to observations of hydraulic head and cGNSS vertical surface displacement from the same month, together with seasonal terms, and the resulting estimates were compared with the corresponding MLCW measurements. During a six-month delay in MLCW data delivery, the estimates agreed more closely with the observed deformation increments in S1--S4 than in S5 and S6, with $R^2$ values of 0.80--0.98 and MAE values of 0.15--0.26~mm/month in the four shallower sections. The 90\% Bayesian posterior predictive intervals contained 65.9--88.4\% of the observations during delayed delivery, and average coverage remained below 90\% in every scenario with less frequent or no later MLCW measurements.

When MLCW measurements were recorded every six or twelve months, average monthly MAE remained within 0.27--0.31~mm/month across the tested combinations of measurement interval and length of the initial record. In comparisons with delayed delivery over the same 72 months, four of the six scenarios provided no clear direction of change, whereas MAE was higher by 0.015 and 0.024~mm/month in the two scenarios based on the 8-year initial record, with resampling intervals indicating higher error in both cases. The comparison changed when monthly discrepancies were accumulated to the next MLCW measurement, with endpoint MAE of 1.4--1.54~mm at six months and 2.47--2.74~mm at twelve months; these values reflect the longer period over which discrepancies could accumulate before a direct MLCW observation became available. When no later MLCW measurements were available, monthly MAE remained within 0.26--0.29~mm/month for the initial records of 3, 5, and 8 years. Average absolute cumulative error after 80 months ranged from 4.29 to 7.39~mm, and the longest initial record did not produce the lowest cumulative error throughout the evaluation period or in every depth section. The difference between monthly and accumulated errors shows that monthly observations of hydraulic head and cGNSS surface displacement can support estimates for individual months, whereas direct MLCW measurements remain necessary to evaluate deformation accumulated within each depth section. The framework uses monthly error, accumulated discrepancy, and posterior interval coverage to compare candidate schedules at each site rather than prescribe a universal interval. Independent evaluation at other MLCW stations and a fuller account of uncertainty would show whether the same approach can support monthly deformation monitoring across individual depth sections beyond Tuku.
