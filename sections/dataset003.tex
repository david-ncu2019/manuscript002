\subsection{Datasets}
\label{subsec:datasets}

Deformation within individual subsurface depth intervals was measured monthly using specialized borehole extensometer systems known as multilayer compaction monitoring wells (MLCWs). Although the field measurements were collected monthly, the data provider processed and checked the measurements before providing the finalized records. Once provided, however, the finalized records contained the complete sequence of monthly observations collected during the intervening period. The delay therefore affected data availability rather than measurement frequency. To represent this temporary information gap consistently, the analysis treated the six most recent months of MLCW observations as unavailable. The six-month interval defined the evaluation setting and did not represent a fixed data delivery schedule.

The Tuku MLCW station was selected for its location within an actively subsiding agricultural area and for the availability of the required observations. Tuku Township lies in the agricultural region of Yunlin County and within a major inland subsidence zone of the CRAF \citep{chang2022_wetanddry, hung_measuring_2021, hung_exploring_2024}. The station provided the complete set of observations needed to evaluate the monitoring scenarios considered in this study. Three time-series data sources formed the monthly regression dataset: (1) MLCW observations, (2) groundwater level (GWL) records, and (3) vertical surface displacement recorded by an adjacent continuous Global Navigation Satellite System (cGNSS) station. A corresponding borehole lithological profile was examined separately to characterize material differences among the six depth sections. The MLCW observations provided monthly compaction increments for individual depth intervals. These increments were estimated from monthly hydraulic head changes and vertical surface displacement increments for the same months. The MLCW and cGNSS records covered January 2010 to December 2024, whereas the GWL record covered January 2008 to December 2024. The following subsections describe the observational basis and temporal alignment of each dataset.

\subsubsection{Multilayer aquifer-system deformation}

The MLCW recorded relative vertical displacement at multiple depths through magnetic anchor rings installed along the borehole to a depth of approximately 300~m. Relative displacement between adjacent rings represented compaction within the corresponding ring intervals, with a measurement precision of 1~mm \citep{hung_measuring_2021}. Because the field measurements were collected on different calendar days, successive observations did not cover common monthly intervals. The displacement series were therefore aligned to common monthly epochs using the deformation time series model described in \Cref{subsec:deformation_model}. Because the ring intervals did not coincide with uniform depth boundaries, the aligned deformation profiles were then represented by six standardized 50~m depth sections, designated S1 through S6. Differences between consecutive monthly values provided the monthly compaction increment within each depth section. The station code, monitored depth, and sampling interval are summarized in \Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}.

\subsubsection{Groundwater level observations}

GWL observations described variations in hydraulic head associated with aquifer-system compaction and land subsidence in alluvial fan settings \citep{galloway_land_1999, gambolati_2015, herrera-garcia_mapping_2021, bagheri-gavkoshLandSubsidenceGlobal2021}. The regional GWL monitoring network comprised over 90 groundwater level monitoring stations and was operated by the Water Resources Agency of Taiwan. These stations generally consisted of nested observation wells screened at discrete depths to monitor hydraulic head in Aquifers 1 through 4 \citep{survey_project_1999, liu_characterization_2004}. Within this network, the Tuku station contained three observation wells screened over depth intervals of 81--84, 176--179, and 257--263~m. Daily piezometric head measurements from these wells were reported as elevations relative to mean sea level (m MSL). These records captured seasonal drawdown associated with agricultural pumping, recovery during the wet season, and longer-term changes in hydraulic head \citep{chang2022_wetanddry, lu2020_crfp}. To place the GWL observations on the same monthly time scale as the MLCW measurements, daily hydraulic heads were averaged within each month. Differences between consecutive monthly means then provided the monthly hydraulic head changes used in the analysis (\Cref{subsec:predictor_preparation}). The monitoring configuration and sampling interval are summarized in \Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}.

\subsubsection{Vertical surface displacement}

Vertical surface displacement represented vertical deformation integrated over the depth of the underlying aquifer system. Whereas the Tuku MLCW recorded deformation only to its deepest magnetic ring, installed at a depth of approximately 300~m, surface displacement also included deformation below this monitored depth \citep{hung_measuring_2021}. Daily three-dimensional position time series from the adjacent TKJS cGNSS station provided the surface displacement observations used at Tuku \citep{IESAS_TGM_2026}. Like the MLCW records, the cGNSS series was aligned to common monthly epochs using the deformation time series model described in \Cref{subsec:deformation_model}. Differences between consecutive monthly values then provided the vertical surface displacement increment for each month. The station code and sampling interval are summarized in \Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}.

\subsubsection{Borehole lithological profile}

The borehole lithological profile provided with the Tuku MLCW records described variations in sediment composition with depth along the borehole. The source log identified the upper and lower boundaries of each logged interval and its corresponding sediment type. Because these intervals did not coincide with the six standardized 50~m depth sections, sediment thicknesses were aggregated within each section. Sediment composition within each standardized section was then expressed as the proportions of gravel, coarse sand, fine sand, and fine-grained deposits comprising clay, silt, and mud. These proportions characterized lithological differences among the six depth sections and provided geological context for interpreting differences in deformation and model performance among sections.

Together, the three time-series datasets linked monthly compaction within each 50~m depth section to corresponding hydraulic head changes and vertical surface displacement. The MLCW increments provided the monthly response, while hydraulic head changes and surface displacement increments provided the time-varying information used for estimation. After temporal alignment, these records formed the monthly regression dataset summarized in \Cref{fig:tuku_data_workflow}. The lithological profile remained separate from the model inputs and provided geological context for interpreting differences among depth sections. The following section describes how the monthly variables were assembled and used to estimate compaction with Bayesian ridge regression.
