\subsection{Datasets}
\label{subsec:datasets}

% AUTHOR-IDEA RESOLVED [DATA-OVERVIEW-01]: Integrated the reduced-monitoring rationale, target vs. predictor roles, and dataset overview into a single cohesive paragraph.
Compaction within individual subsurface depth intervals was measured monthly using specialized borehole extensometer systems known as multilayer compaction monitoring wells (MLCWs). However, long-term operation of the MLCW network required continued funding for regular well maintenance and repeated field measurements. Consequently, this study considered a future monitoring schedule under which the MLCW stations would remain operational, but measurements would be collected only once every six to twelve months to reduce monitoring costs. Under this scenario, monthly compaction between successive MLCW measurements was estimated from other monitoring data. Four data sources were integrated for this purpose, comprising (1) MLCW observations, (2) groundwater level (GWL) records, (3) borehole lithological profiles, and (4) vertical surface displacement derived from cGNSS observations and SBAS-InSAR analysis. MLCW observations provided the reference compaction measurements. Groundwater level changes, vertical surface displacement, and sediment composition across depths and locations were used for monthly estimation. Previous MLCW measurements were not used as model inputs. The following subsections describe each dataset and the temporal alignment applied before model construction.

\subsubsection{Multilayer aquifer-system compaction}

MLCWs measured compaction at multiple depths using magnetic anchor rings installed along each borehole. Each MLCW contained 21 to 26 rings installed to a maximum depth of 300~m. Displacement between adjacent rings resolved compaction within the corresponding depth intervals with a measurement precision of 1~mm \citep{hung_measuring_2021}. Monthly observations were obtained from five MLCW stations (Guangfu, Huwei, Tuku, Honglun, and Xiutan). Station coordinates and monitoring specifications are summarized in \Cref{tab:mlcw_info}.

% AUTHOR-IDEA RESOLVED [DATA-OVERVIEW-02]: Explained why the observations required temporal alignment before monthly differencing.
The field measurements were not collected on a common day, so observations from different stations were collected on different dates within the same month. Each displacement series between adjacent magnetic rings was therefore fitted with a deformation time series model comprising linear, periodic, and step functions (\Cref{subsec:parametric_deformation}). After model fitting, the series were evaluated at common monthly epochs. Because magnetic ring depths differed among boreholes, the resulting deformation profiles were represented by six uniform 50~m depth sections, designated S1 through S6 over the 0--300~m depth range. Finally, differences between consecutive monthly section values were calculated to obtain the compaction increment for each depth section.

\subsubsection{Groundwater level observations}

Groundwater level (GWL) observations recorded variations in hydraulic head associated with aquifer-system compaction and land subsidence in alluvial fan aquifer systems \citep{galloway_land_1999, gambolati_2015, herrera-garcia_mapping_2021, bagheri-gavkoshLandSubsidenceGlobal2021}. The regional GWL monitoring network operated by the Water Resources Agency (WRA) of Taiwan comprised nested observation wells screened at discrete depth intervals corresponding to Aquifers 1 through 4 \citep{survey_project_1999, liu_characterization_2004}. Daily piezometric head measurements, expressed as elevations relative to Mean Sea Level (m MSL), spanned from January 2000 to December 2025 across Yunlin County, capturing seasonal agricultural pumping drawdowns, monsoon recharge during the wet season, and head declines over multiple decades \citep{chang2022_wetanddry, lu2020_crfp}. Station locations, screened intervals, and monitoring periods are summarized in \Cref{tab:gwl_info}. The daily observations were averaged within each month to place hydraulic head on the same temporal support as the MLCW response. Differences between consecutive monthly means were then calculated to obtain monthly hydraulic head changes (\Cref{subsec:hydrogeological_drivers}).

\subsubsection{Borehole lithological profiles}

Borehole lithological profiles described variations in sediment composition among the six 50~m compaction sections. Detailed logs at each MLCW recorded the vertical distribution of gravel, sand, silt, clay, and mixed fine-grained deposits to depths of 300~m. The logged materials were classified as gravel, coarse sand, fine sand, or a combined clay, silt, and mud group within each depth section. Unlike hydraulic head and displacement, these compositions did not vary through time. They allowed the model to distinguish sediment conditions among depth sections and monitoring locations.

% AUTHOR-IDEA RESOLVED [DATA-OVERVIEW-03]: Added the geological role of the sediment fractions and the reason for the compositional transformation.
The four sediment fractions summed to a constant, so their direct inclusion would introduce exact linear dependence among the predictor variables. The fractions were therefore expressed as isometric logratio balances that retained their relative information without the closure constraint (\Cref{subsec:ilr_sbp_transformation}). The resulting balances provided the sediment variables used in model calibration and evaluation at the monitoring stations.

% AUTHOR-IDEA RESOLVED [DATA-OVERVIEW-04]: The existing data-source citation provides the website reference for the regional model.
Sediment architecture beyond the monitoring stations was represented separately by the 3D hydrogeological model developed by the Geological Survey and Mining Management Agency \citep{gsmma_3d}. The regional model provided continuous lithological profiles at a 1~m vertical resolution and a 500~m horizontal grid spacing. These profiles extended the description of sediment composition to locations without MLCW observations and supported estimation across the study area. They did not replace the borehole-derived compositions used in analyses at the monitoring stations.

\subsubsection{Vertical surface displacement}

Vertical surface displacement measurements recorded the combined surface expression of deformation occurring throughout the subsurface. The MLCWs measured compaction only to the depth of their deepest magnetic rings, installed at depths of approximately 300~m \citep{hung_measuring_2021}. By contrast, vertical surface displacement also included compaction below this monitored depth. Daily 3D position time series from continuous GNSS (cGNSS) stations located at or near the MLCW sites \citep{IESAS_TGM_2026} provided point observations of vertical displacement from 2010 to 2024, with station coordinates and monitoring periods summarized in \Cref{tab:gnss_info}. These cGNSS records provided the surface displacement observations used in analyses at the MLCW stations.

Surface locations outside the cGNSS network were monitored using Small Baseline Subset (SBAS) InSAR analysis of Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery, comprising 266 ascending path 69 images and 264 descending path 105 images with acquisition parameters summarized in \Cref{tab:sentinel1_info} \citep{torres_gmes_2012, yague-martinez_interferometric_2016}. SBAS interferogram stacks were generated with the HyP3 pipeline \citep{hogenson_hybrid_2025}, and multitemporal phase inversion was conducted using MintPy \citep{yunjun_small_2019}. These processing steps produced initial displacement measurements along the radar line of sight. Vertical surface displacement was then resolved from the ascending and descending observations by vector decomposition in two dimensions \citep{fuhrmann_resolving_2019, hanssen_radar_2001}. The resulting SBAS-InSAR observations provided surface displacement across the study area for regional estimation.

The cGNSS and SBAS-InSAR time series were subsequently represented at monthly epochs using the deformation model comprising linear, periodic, and step functions (\Cref{subsec:parametric_deformation}). Differences between consecutive monthly values were calculated to obtain vertical surface displacement increments.

Together, the four data sources established a common framework for estimating monthly compaction. The MLCW increments defined the response, hydraulic head changes and vertical surface displacement described variation through time, and the lithological profiles distinguished sediment composition among locations and depth sections. Their integration produced a monthly dataset aligned in space and time across the six 50~m depth intervals (\Cref{fig:preprocessing_workflow}).
