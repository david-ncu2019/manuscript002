\subsection{Datasets}
\label{subsec:datasets}

Estimating deformation within individual depth intervals required observations of deformation by depth together with monthly hydraulic head and surface displacement records. The Tuku MLCW station provided this combination of observations within an actively subsiding agricultural area. Tuku Township lies in the agricultural region of Yunlin County and within a major inland subsidence zone of the CRAF \citep{chang2022_wetanddry, hung_measuring_2021, hung_exploring_2024}. The station was therefore selected for both its physical setting and the completeness of the records needed to evaluate the monitoring scenarios considered in this study.

Three time-series data sources formed the monthly dataset. MLCW observations provided deformation increments within individual depth intervals, while monthly hydraulic head changes and vertical surface displacement increments provided the corresponding information used to estimate them. The temporal variation in these records is shown in \Cref{fig:tuku_observations_b}. The surface displacement was recorded at an adjacent cGNSS station. A borehole lithological profile was examined separately to characterize material differences among the six depth sections. The MLCW and cGNSS records covered January 2010 to December 2024, whereas the GWL record covered January 2008 to December 2024.

MLCW measurements were collected every month and then processed and checked by the data provider before release. Each release contained the complete sequence of monthly observations collected since the preceding release. The delay therefore affected when the finalized records became available, not how often measurements were made. The following subsections describe the datasets and their temporal alignment, while \Cref{subsec:delayed_evaluation} describes how monthly deformation was estimated before the finalized MLCW records became available.

\subsubsection{Multilayer aquifer-system deformation}

The MLCW measured relative vertical displacement at multiple depths using magnetic anchor rings installed along the borehole to approximately 300~m. Displacement between adjacent rings represented deformation within the corresponding depth intervals, with a measurement precision of 1~mm \citep{hung_measuring_2021}. Because the field measurements were collected on different calendar days, successive observations did not cover common monthly intervals. The displacement series was therefore aligned to common monthly dates using the deformation time series model described in \Cref{subsec:deformation_model}.

The intervals between magnetic rings also differed from the uniform depth boundaries used in the analysis. The aligned deformation profiles were therefore represented by six standardized 50~m depth sections, labeled S1 through S6. Differences between consecutive monthly values provided the monthly deformation increment within each section. The station code, monitored depth, and sampling interval are summarized in \Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}.

\subsubsection{Groundwater level observations}

GWL observations provided hydraulic head records from aquifers monitored at different depths. These records represented groundwater conditions associated with aquifer-system deformation and land subsidence in alluvial fan settings \citep{galloway_land_1999, gambolati_2015, herrera-garcia_mapping_2021, bagheri-gavkoshLandSubsidenceGlobal2021}. The study area included 95 GWL monitoring stations operated by the Water Resources Agency of Taiwan \citep{nguyen_quantitative_2024}. These stations generally contained nested observation wells screened at discrete depths within Aquifers 1 through 4 \citep{survey_project_1999, liu_characterization_2004}. At Tuku, three observation wells were screened at depths of 81--84, 176--179, and 257--263~m. Daily piezometric heads from these wells were reported as elevations relative to mean sea level (m MSL).

The hydraulic head records captured seasonal drawdown associated with agricultural pumping, recovery during the wet season, and longer-term changes \citep{chang2022_wetanddry, lu2020_crfp}. To place these observations on the same monthly time scale as the MLCW measurements, daily hydraulic heads were averaged within each month. Differences between consecutive monthly means then provided the monthly hydraulic head changes used in the analysis (\Cref{subsec:predictor_preparation}). The monitoring configuration and sampling interval are summarized in \Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}.

\subsubsection{Vertical surface displacement}

Vertical surface displacement represented vertical deformation integrated over the depth of the underlying aquifer system. Whereas the Tuku MLCW recorded deformation only to its deepest magnetic ring, installed at a depth of approximately 300~m, surface displacement also included deformation below this monitored depth \citep{hung_measuring_2021}. Daily three-dimensional position time series from the adjacent TKJS cGNSS station provided the surface displacement observations used at Tuku \citep{IESAS_TGM_2026}. Like the MLCW records, the cGNSS series was aligned to common monthly epochs using the deformation time series model described in \Cref{subsec:deformation_model}. Differences between consecutive monthly values then provided the vertical surface displacement increment for each month. The station code and sampling interval are summarized in \Cref{tab:tuku_data_sources}.

\subsubsection{Borehole lithological profile}

The borehole lithological profile provided with the Tuku MLCW records described variations in sediment composition with depth along the borehole. The source log identified the upper and lower boundaries of each logged interval and its corresponding sediment type. As in the alignment of the MLCW deformation record (\Cref{subsec:deformation_model}), these logged intervals did not coincide with the six standardized 50~m depth sections used throughout the analysis. Sediment thicknesses were therefore aggregated within each 50~m section so that lithological composition could be compared directly against the deformation estimated for that same section. Sediment composition within each standardized section was then expressed as the proportions of gravel, coarse sand, fine sand, and fine-grained deposits comprising clay, silt, and mud. The resulting composition of each depth section is reported in \Cref{supp-tab:supp_tuku_lithology_by_section}. These proportions characterized lithological differences among the six depth sections and provided geological context for interpreting differences in deformation and model performance among sections. The profile is shown alongside cumulative MLCW deformation in \Cref{fig:tuku_observations_a}.

The three aligned time-series datasets connected monthly deformation within each 50~m depth section with corresponding changes in hydraulic head and vertical surface displacement. The MLCW increments provided the deformation values to be estimated, while hydraulic head changes and surface displacement increments provided the monthly information used for that estimation. These records formed the dataset summarized in \Cref{fig:tuku_data_workflow}. The lithological profile remained separate from the model and provided geological context for interpreting differences among depth sections.
